## 📘 Course Description

This course develops students’ ability to write clear, logical, evidence-based English essays, with a strong focus on **exposition and argumentation**. Students learn how to explain current issues, take reasoned positions, build arguments, evaluate evidence, respond to counter-arguments, and avoid common logical fallacies.

In the 2026–2027 course, writing topics will be more closely connected with **current affairs and everyday life**, especially issues in society, science, technology, education, public health, environment, and media. Students will practice writing about real-world questions such as public health responsibility, social media restrictions for young people, AI in education, online privacy, environmental protection, and the social effects of new technologies.

The course also incorporates the use of **AI tools** into regular writing practice. Students are allowed to use AI tools for brainstorming, outlining, language revision, counter-argument generation, and feedback. However, students must use AI tools **strategically, critically, responsibly, and transparently**. AI may support the writing process, but it must not replace students’ own thinking, reasoning, evidence, organization, or final expression.

---

## 🧠 Critical Thinking Skills Practiced

Throughout the course, students will practice:

* identifying claims, reasons, evidence, assumptions, and conclusions;
* distinguishing fact, opinion, inference, and assumption;
* applying intellectual standards such as clarity, precision, accuracy, relevance, consistency, logical correctness, completeness, and fairness;
* evaluating source credibility, evidence quality, and reasoning strength;
* recognizing bias, overgeneralization, weak evidence, and logical fallacies;
* constructing and testing counter-arguments;
* making reasoned judgments: accept, reject, or suspend judgment;
* translating critical reading and analysis into coherent paragraphs and full essays;
* using AI tools as thinking partners while maintaining independent judgment.

---

## 🤖 AI-Supported Writing in This Course

AI tools will be used as part of writing practice, but they must be used with clear purpose and awareness.

Students may use AI tools to:

* brainstorm possible angles for an essay topic;
* generate questions about a current issue;
* compare possible thesis statements;
* test whether a paragraph has a clear controlling idea;
* ask for feedback on coherence and organization;
* generate possible counter-arguments;
* identify possible weaknesses in reasoning;
* check grammar, clarity, or academic tone;
* revise language after the student has written a draft.

Students may **not** use AI tools to:

* write the whole assignment without personal contribution;
* replace their own thesis, reasoning, or evidence;
* submit AI-generated text as their own original writing;
* invent sources or facts;
* use information without checking accuracy;
* avoid reading, thinking, drafting, or revising.

**Key principle**

AI may help you think, test, revise, and polish.
But you remain the writer.

</div>

## 🇨🇳 思政融入

## Ideological and Civic Literacy Integration

This course integrates language learning, critical thinking, and civic responsibility.

Students will:

* discuss real issues in society, education, technology, health, and public life;
* develop the ability to make responsible value judgments based on evidence;
* practice rational expression and respectful disagreement;
* learn to avoid stereotypes, emotional manipulation, and fallacious reasoning;
* understand academic integrity, responsible AI use, and transparent source use;
* connect personal learning with broader social responsibility;
* consider both Chinese and global perspectives on public issues such as education, public health, technological change, and sustainable development.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

1. Write coherent paragraphs and essays with clear organization, development, and cohesion.

2. Use expository and argumentative writing strategies, including explanation, comparison, definition, cause-effect reasoning, claims, evidence, counter-arguments, and rebuttals.

3. Analyze and evaluate arguments by identifying claims, reasons, assumptions, evidence, logical structure, and possible fallacies.

4. Locate, evaluate, integrate, and cite sources responsibly through summary, paraphrase, quotation, and evidence-based discussion.

5. Use rhetorical strategies such as ethos, pathos, and logos appropriately in academic writing.

6. Use AI tools strategically, transparently, and critically during brainstorming, drafting, revision, and reflection.

7. Write clear, accurate, and well-organized English essays on current social, scientific, technological, educational, and public-life issues.

## ✍️ Major Writing Assignments

### Essay 1: Exposition-Supported Opinion Essay

This essay asks students to explain a current issue and present a clear opinion.

Possible topics:

* Why do some countries restrict social media use for people under 16?
* Is disease prevention mainly an individual responsibility or a collective responsibility?
* Why has sleep become a public health concern among university students?
* Why should university students learn AI literacy?
* Why is online misinformation difficult to control?

Required skills:

* clear topic and controlling idea;
* effective exposition through illustration, comparison, definition, or cause-effect explanation;
* relevant examples from current affairs or everyday life;
* at least one credible source;
* clear paragraph organization;
* transparent AI Use Statement, if AI tools are used.

---

### Essay 2: Argumentative Essay

This essay asks students to take a clear position on a debatable issue and defend it with reasoning and evidence.

Possible topics:

* Should social media platforms be banned for users under 16?
* Should universities allow students to use AI tools in writing courses?
* Should disease prevention depend mainly on personal choice or public policy?
* Should algorithmic recommendation systems be more strictly regulated?
* Should schools limit students’ use of smartphones?
* Should public health campaigns rely more on education or regulation?

Required skills:

* arguable thesis;
* clear claims and reasons;
* credible evidence;
* logical organization;
* counter-argument and rebuttal;
* fair treatment of opposing views;
* source citation;
* transparent AI Use Statement, if AI tools are used.

Major essays will be evaluated according to the following criteria:

| Criterion                 | Focus                                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Thesis / controlling idea | Is the main idea clear, focused, and arguable where appropriate?                               |
| Development               | Are claims explained with reasons, examples, evidence, and analysis?                           |
| Organization              | Are paragraphs unified, coherent, and logically ordered?                                       |
| Evidence and source use   | Are sources credible, relevant, accurately represented, and properly cited?                    |

## 📜 Course Policies

### 1. Attendance and Participation

Active engagement is expected.

Students should:

* attend class regularly;
* arrive on time;
* participate in discussions and workshops;
* complete in-class writing tasks;
* give responsible peer feedback.

Unexcused absences lower the final grade. More than three unexcused absences may result in a failing grade. Three late arrivals count as one absence.

---

### 2. Submission and Late Work

Students should submit assignments on time and follow instructions carefully.

Late work policy:

* Late work may lose one grade step per day.
* Work submitted more than seven days late may not be accepted.
* Technical problems are not automatically accepted as excuses.
* Students should save drafts and plan ahead.

---

### 3. Academic Integrity

Plagiarism and unauthorized collaboration are prohibited.

Students must not:

* copy another student’s work;
* copy from online sources without citation;
* submit AI-generated writing as their own;
* invent sources, evidence, or data;
* use translation or AI tools in ways that replace their own work.

Academic misconduct may result in a grade of zero for the assignment or further disciplinary action.

---

### 4. Responsible AI Use

AI tools are allowed in this course for appropriate learning and writing support.

However, students must:

* remain the primary author of their work;
* make their own decisions about thesis, structure, reasoning, and evidence;
* verify facts and sources;
* rewrite AI-supported language in their own words;
* disclose AI use when required;
* submit an AI Use Statement for major assignments if AI tools were used.

Submitting AI-generated text as original student writing is misconduct.
